01 — Brand Strategy Summary

A national digital backbone — not a product, an institution.

NileGov is the orchestration layer for public service delivery in Uganda. The brand must read as state‑grade infrastructure: secure, calm, permanent, and unmistakably Ugandan — while still feeling like modern enterprise software that civil servants and citizens actually want to use.

Positioning

Uganda's public digital backbone

The connective tissue between every ministry, department, agency and district — one identity, one standard, many services.

Promise

Trusted by design

Every interaction is secure, auditable and accountable. The brand signals stability before it signals novelty.

Tension resolved

Authority × Access

Institutional enough for a Permanent Secretary; legible and warm enough for a citizen at a sub‑county office.

02 — Creative Inspiration Lens

The work is read through four disciplines, held in deliberate tension.

Strategic brand architecture (Landor) gives the system its hierarchy and meaning. Digital product elegance (AKQA) keeps it from feeling like a static logo and ties it to a living interface. Design‑system rigor (Design Bridge & Partners) ensures every asset is governed, tokenised and reproducible. And public‑sector usability discipline (GOV.UK, Singapore GovTech / USWDS, e‑Estonia) keeps the brand accountable to the citizen first — legible, accessible, never decorative for its own sake.

03 — Three Logo Routes

Three defensible directions, one common spine.

Every route is built from the same monogram logic — the letter N whose diagonal is the Nile, a continuous flow of public value moving through the structure of the state. They differ in what they emphasise: authority, systems, or access.

ROUTE A

Sentinel

Institutional · Authoritative · Government‑grade

Concept. A civic gateway. The monogram sits inside a rounded structural frame — a portal every public service passes through. The N's diagonal is a tapered river (source narrow, delta wide), and a red rule grounds it as the constitutional foundation.

Why it works. A solid framed mark survives at 16px, in a single colour, embossed on paper or pressed into a seal. It reads as permanent state infrastructure without copying the coat of arms. Best for: official portal, document & print headers, ministry co‑branding.

Route A — Sentinel · the framed civic gateway
Route B — Meridian · the connected service network
ROUTE B

Meridian

Digital Infrastructure · Systems‑driven · Technology‑forward

Concept. The N drawn as a network. Four gold nodes (MDAs, districts, services) are linked by a flowing diagonal data‑path; a single red node at the centre is the live citizen transaction moving through the system. Built on an explicit modular grid.

Why it works. It tells the ERP story directly — connected, orchestrated, scalable. Best for: developer & platform contexts, dashboards, technical documentation, integration / API surfaces. Risk: node detail can soften at very small sizes.

ROUTE C

Threshold

Citizen Services · Accessible · Warm‑but‑official

Concept. A doorway to public services. A rounded gold arch (the open gateway) holds a flowing Nile curve across its threshold; the negative space reads as a soft N. Geometry is rounder and more human.

Why it works. It lowers the barrier — it invites rather than commands. Best for: public‑facing citizen portals, service kiosks, outreach & onboarding. Risk: can read slightly less "state authority" on its own.

Route C — Threshold · the civic gateway / doorway

04 — Selected Winning Route

Selected · Route A — Sentinel

Sentinel wins on the only criteria that matter for the state.

For a national governance platform, the deciding tests are permanence, reproducibility and small‑size survival — not visual novelty. Sentinel is the only route that holds as a one‑colour seal on a printed certificate, as a 16px favicon, and as an embossed mark, while still carrying the full meaning: a structured gateway, the Nile as continuous public value, and a red foundation of law.

Meridian's network and Threshold's arch are excellent expressions of the same idea and are retained as a secondary motif language (used in dashboards and citizen‑portal art). But the master mark must be the most robust and the most institutional. That is Sentinel.

05 — Detailed Final Logo Description

Anatomy of the Sentinel mark

The frameRounded structural square = the civic gateway and the container of the state. Permanent, symmetric, contained.
The uprightsTwo clean pillars in paper‑white = institutions / pillars of governance, and the literal strokes of the N.
The diagonalA tapered gold band — the Nile. Narrow at the source, widening toward the delta = public value flowing and growing as it moves through the system.
The ruleA thin red baseline = the constitutional / legal foundation and the line of accountability.
ConstructionBuilt on a 128‑unit grid; corner radius and stroke weights are proportional so the mark scales without redrawing.
Clear space = the height of one upright on every side. Minimum size: 20px (digital), 8mm (print). Never place the mark on a busy photograph without the dark or paper containment plate.

10 — Typography System

One open‑source family. State‑ready, free to deploy.

The brand and UI typeface is IBM Plex Sans, with IBM Plex Mono for data, IDs and audit trails. Chosen deliberately: institutional pedigree, excellent legibility, broad Latin coverage for local languages, and an SIL Open Font License — no licensing cost or risk across an entire government estate.

11 — Visual Identity System

Iconography

Monoline, 1.75px stroke on a 24px grid, rounded joins, square‑ish silhouettes echoing the frame. Single weight; never duotone or filled‑illustrative.

Grid & spacing

8‑point spatial system. Generous, calm density on citizen surfaces; tighter on staff surfaces. 12‑column responsive content grid.

Pattern / motif

The "Meridian" node‑network as a faint background texture (≤6% opacity) — the connected‑services story, used as art, never behind body text.

Illustration

Geometric, line‑led, built from the same stroke language. Diverse, dignified depictions of Ugandans accessing services — never clip‑art or generic stock metaphors.

Photography

Documentary, natural light, real civil servants and citizens. Warm but truthful colour grade. A consistent gold or ink duotone overlay for hero use.

Motion

Calm and purposeful. The signature gesture: the gold diagonal drawing along its flow path (200–320ms, ease‑out). No bounce, no spin. Respects reduced‑motion.

The "seal" motif (design element — not an official seal)

A circular containment motif for certificates, verified documents and stamps. It deliberately avoids the national coat of arms and any official state seal — it is a brand graphic that signals authenticity and verification, paired with the mono reference number and verification hash from the type system.

Use only on issued / verified artefacts. It is never a substitute for the legally mandated seals of state.

12 — Usage Guidelines & Do / Don't

✓ Do

  • Keep one full upright of clear space around the mark.
  • Use the mono‑white mark on photography, always on a dark plate.
  • Let one colour lead per surface — usually ink, with a single gold accent.
  • Use IBM Plex Mono for every reference number and ID.
  • Co‑brand with agencies using the equal‑weight hairline lockup.
  • Reserve red for foundation, action and error only.

✕ Don't

  • Recolour the mark outside the four brand colours.
  • Set gold as text on a white background (fails contrast).
  • Stretch, rotate, add gradients, shadows or 3‑D to the mark.
  • Use all three flag colours at equal weight (no flag collage).
  • Imitate the coat of arms or any official state seal.
  • Place the mark on a busy image without a containment plate.
One‑line brand rationale

NileGov is the structure of the state with the Nile of public value flowing through it — permanent, secure and proudly Ugandan.

14 — Frappe / ERPNext Implementation Checklist

Exactly how to apply NileGov in Frappe v15.

A developer‑ready sequence. Most lives in a custom app's hooks.py, a brand CSS bundle, and the Website Settings / Navbar Settings doctypes.

  1. App identity & logoSet app_logo_url, app_name="NileGov" and app_title in hooks.py. Upload the SVG mark to /assets/nilegov/img/.
  2. Favicon & theme‑colorWebsite Settings → Favicon = favicon.svg. Add <meta name="theme-color" content="#111315"> and the maskable / apple‑touch icons via web_include_html.
  3. Splash / loadingOverride the Frappe splash with the mono‑white mark on ink; add the gold‑diagonal draw animation in the brand CSS, gated on prefers-reduced-motion.
  4. Login screenSet login_with_email_link branding; override login.html / inject CSS for the tri‑colour top rule, paper card, ink button with gold accent bar.
  5. Navbar & top barNavbar Settings → brand HTML = compact mark + "NileGov". Brand CSS: ink background, 2px gold bottom border, neutral avatar.
  6. Desk theme tokensMap CSS variables (--primary, --text-color, sidebar, card) to the NileGov palette in a app_include_css bundle; set active‑nav and focus states to gold.
  7. Workspace & module iconsReplace default module icons with the monoline 24px set; apply the 3px left‑rule colour logic (gold / red / slate) to workspace cards.
  8. Forms, tables & reports8px field radius, gold focus ring, red validation only; force IBM Plex Mono on .dt-cell numeric / ID columns; highlight the single key metric in gold.
  9. Print formatsBuild a NileGov Letterhead (tri‑colour rule, "Republic of Uganda / NileGov" lockup, mono ref number, red footer rule); set as default Print Format header.
  10. Email templatesOverride the standard email header: ink band, reversed‑white mark, gold CTA button, plain‑language body, mono ref in the footer.
  11. System & notification statesStandardise toast / alert colours: gold = info, red = action required, green = complete — each with an icon, never colour alone.
  12. FontsSelf‑host IBM Plex Sans + Mono under /assets/nilegov/fonts/ (OFL); declare @font-face and set the global font stack — no external CDN dependency for a government deployment.
  13. Brand modesExpose a body class (.mode-portal / .mode-staff / .mode-citizen) toggled by role / site, swapping density, descriptor and motif per the three‑mode spec.
  14. GovernanceCommit the tokens, SVGs, fonts and CSS as a versioned nilegov_branding app so every site, ministry and district inherits one source of truth.
